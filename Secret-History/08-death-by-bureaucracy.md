# Secret History #8: Death by Bureaucracy
How the Bureaucratic Mindset Is Taking Over the World

## 1. The 2015 Yale Halloween Incident

In October 2015, Yale’s Intercultural Affairs Committee sent an email to students before Halloween:

> “Please be sensitive about the feelings of other people. Have fun, but avoid costumes that might offend others.”

Erika Christakis, Associate Master of Silliman College (one of Yale’s residential colleges), responded to her students with a counter-email arguing that university is a place for exploration, experimentation, and even making mistakes. She wrote that while sensitivity matters, adults should be trusted to navigate difficult moments and recover from them.

This sparked a campus-wide debate:

- **Safe space** (the university’s position): Feelings must be protected above all.
- **Free space** (Christakis’s position): Intellectual growth requires discomfort, debate, and occasional hurt feelings.

Most students in the class said they preferred safe space. The lecturer notes this represents a generational shift: when he was young, free space was the unquestioned default.

## 2. The Confrontations

When Nicholas Christakis (Erika’s husband and Master of Silliman) attended a campus meeting, he was surrounded by approximately 100 angry students who accused the couple of racism and demanded an apology. One widely circulated video shows a female student shouting at him:

> “It is your job to create a place of comfort and home for the students… By sending out that email, that goes against your position as Master… Then why the fuck did you accept the position?”

Nicholas Christakis calmly replied that his job was to help students learn and grow, which sometimes involves pain and challenge. The student rejected this, insisting the role was to make students “feel good” and “at home.”

The lecturer’s observation:  
Both sides had legitimate points, but the students’ contempt and cursing toward a professor was unimaginable in earlier generations.

## 4. The Real Explanation: Universities Have Become Bureaucracies

Once universities become prestigious global brands, the struggle is over. Administrators no longer need to focus on teaching or research. Instead, they engage in **rent-seeking**: expanding their own salaries, perks, and staff while the core mission atrophies.

## 6. Theoretical Framework

### Franz Kafka – The Trial (pre-WWI)

Bureaucracies must invent meaningless work to justify their existence. They prefer harassing the innocent (who comply) over confronting real problems (which might involve risk).

### Hannah Arendt – The Origins of Totalitarianism

Totalitarian movements are characterised by:

1. Removal from reality
2. Logic of perpetual movement/expansion as proof of truth
3. Defiance of reality as the ultimate test of faith

→ All bureaucracies eventually develop these traits because only growth justifies their existence.

### James C. Scott – Seeing Like a State

States require legibility and simplification. They destroy organic diversity to impose mechanical order:

- German scientific forestry (19th century): replaced diverse forests with monoculture tree farms → initially higher yields, then catastrophic collapse from disease/weather
- Tanzanian forced villagization: moved farmers into planned settlements for “efficiency” → mass starvation
- Cities, wages, private property, resource centralization → all designed to make populations taxable and controllable

Result: society becomes brittle, less resilient, more vulnerable to shocks.

- Collapse of faith in democracy → people feel they have no real influence
- Middle-class life becomes almost impossible for new entrants
