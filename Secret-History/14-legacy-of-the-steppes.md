# Secret History #14: Legacy of the Steppes

## Introduction: The Traditional View Is Completely Backwards

Traditional education teaches that **civilization** is superior in every way:

- Civilization grants **intellectual freedom** through books, schools, and critical thinking
- Civilization is **open, curious, innovative**
- Civilization creates **wealth and happiness**

While the **steppes** (grassland pastoralists, called “barbarians”) are portrayed as:

- Emotional slaves – unpredictable and violent
- Closed-minded and static
- Poor and therefore unhappy

Yet throughout history the “barbarians” repeatedly conquered the civilized empires (Genghis Khan took both China and Baghdad).

**Conclusion of the lecture**: The traditional view is completely wrong.  
It is the **steppes people who are open, curious, aggressive, innovative, and free**.  
It is the **civilized who become close-minded, static, corrupt, and miserable**.

## The Cycle of Civilization

### 1. Origin → City-States (Maximum Innovation)

- People gather around major rivers for religious reasons → temples → settlements → farms
- Strategic trade locations become wealthy cities (e.g. Uruk – first city)
- Population growth → colonization along trade routes → competing city-states
- **Open cooperative competition** (open + cooperation + competition) drives massive innovation

Historical examples of peak innovation during city-state period:

- China → Spring & Autumn / Warring States period (Confucius, Laozi, Sun Tzu, etc.)
- Mesopotamia
- Egypt

The most disadvantaged city is often forced to be the most innovative → conquers the others  
Example: Qin dynasty (mountainous, poor, isolated) conquered the richer Zhou states.

### 2. Empire Phase (Early Boom, Late Stagnation)

Early empire benefits:

- Scale (more resources)
- Standardization (same weights, laws, writing, roads)
- Centralization (massive public works: canals, temples, walls)

→ Tremendous innovation at first

Later empire becomes:

- Insular
- Secretive
- Monopolistic bureaucracy

→ Kills innovation, becomes corrupt

**Exception**: Indus Valley Civilization (modern Pakistan)

- City-state system but remained peaceful, egalitarian, artistic
- No hierarchy, no bureaucracy, no war

### 3. Collapse of Empire

Causes of breakdown:

- Rigid hierarchy/bureaucracy
- Life for ordinary people sucks:  
  – Constant war (conscription)  
  – Debt → debt slavery, selling children  
  – Immobility (tied to the land)
- Elite overproduction → too many elite sons → factional fighting →  
  → Revolution (one elite faction uses the masses against others)  
  → Civil war  
  → External war

When empire weakens, **pastoralists from the steppes conquer it**.

## Why Pastoralists (Steppes People) Always Win

They are the best fighters in the world because:

- Horses + composite bows → horse archers (unbeatable for millennia)
- Constant contact with empires via trade, raiding, mercenary work
- When empire hires them as mercenaries → they often take over (either invited faction loses control or cannot pay them)

## Geography Determines Culture

### Agricultural Societies (River Valleys)

- Original culture (Middle East → Europe):  
  – Matriarchal / women in power  
  – No private property (communal farming)  
  – No money, no war → conflicts solved by discussion  
  – Mother Goddess religion (harmony, compassion, everything sacred)

### Steppes (Grasslands)

Cannot farm → must herd animals (cows, sheep, horses)

Consequences:

- Animals are expensive → private property → money → theft → war
- Need strong men to protect herds → patriarchy
- **Patriarchy + private property + war always appear together**

Major steppe innovations forced by environment:

1. **Lactose tolerance** → drink milk as adults → taller, stronger people
2. **Horse domestication/riding** → mobility, warfare
3. **Wheel + wagon** → mobile households, cover vast steppe

## Steppe Society & Culture

- **Patriarchy** – aggressive, masculine, rewards courage
- **High fertility** (men want many sons for war)
- **Primogeniture** – eldest son inherits everything (keeps family wealthy)  
  → Younger sons must raid/steal cows & women → form secret societies (gangs, warrior bands, later Vikings, etc.)
- **Patron–client relationships** (mafia-style loyalty, not slavery)  
  → No bureaucracy, no debt slavery, or central control  
  → Everyone remains relatively free → superior motivation in war
- **Religion**: Sky God (conquer, exploit, destroy) instead of Mother Goddess  
  → Horses, cattle, war, bravery are sacred

Result: The steppes are a constant **open cooperative competition** system that breeds the fiercest, most innovative warriors. The fiercest stay on the steppe; the ones pushed out conquer empires.

## The Indo-European (Yamnaya) Expansion ~2500 BCE

- Yamnaya culture = classic steppe pastoralists
- Spread west into Europe, south-east into Iran & India
- Evidence:
  DNA + linguistics + archaeology

Linguistic evidence (Proto-Indo-European vocabulary):

- Many words for wheel, wagon, horse, dairy, cattle
- Almost no farming terms
- Common words: father (pater/padar/pitha), mother (mater/madar), two (duo/two), etc.

Conquest patterns:

- Europe → genocide/replacement of local men (Y-chromosome almost totally replaced)
- Spain → all native men killed
- Britain (Stonehenge builders) → completely wiped out
- India → caste system created (upper castes speak Indo-European languages, lower castes Dravidian)

## Old Europe (Pre-Indo-European Farmers)

Marija Gimbutas – “Old Europe” was:

- Peaceful, egalitarian, matristic
- Goddess-centered religion
- Magnificent art, pottery, temples, comfortable villages
- No fortifications, no weapons
- Holistic view: everything sacred, water, stone, animals, plants, sacred
- Writing/symbol systems existed but not used for war

Yamnaya inverted the entire symbolism:

- Snake: life & regeneration → evil (Bible serpent)
- Black: fertility → good; White: bones → death → reversed

## Why Agricultural Societies Collapsed So Easily

1. **Static** – little innovation
2. **Vulnerable to climate change**
3. **Vulnerable to disease/plague** (dense populations + animals)

~4000–3000 BCE plague + climate shift → massive population collapse in Europe → power vacuum → Yamnaya invasion

## The Great Steppe Conquerors (Same Culture, Different Eras)

1. Yamnaya (Proto-Indo-Europeans)
2. Scythians
3. Huns (pushed west by Han Chinese offensive) → triggered migration chain → fall of Rome
4. Turks
5. Mongols (Genghis Khan) – largest contiguous empire ever
6. Timur (Tamerlane) – last great steppe conqueror

All share the same cultural package: horse archers, patron-client tribes, sky god, warrior brotherhoods, primogeniture, extreme mobility, aggressive expansion.

The turning point: **gunpowder** → empires finally conquer the steppes instead of the other way around.

## Final Student Questions

**Student**: Is primogeniture always strict, or could a better son inherit?  
**Lecturer**: General rule is always the eldest son, to avoid murderous conflict among brothers. Even if the eldest is incompetent or manipulated, he still inherits (example: after Genghis Khan's death the empire fragmented because succession became contested).

**Student**: How do steppe people develop religion without temples?  
**Lecturer**: Religion is collective belief/worldview, not buildings. Everyone has a religion. Steppe religion = sky god + horse + cattle + courage + conquest. Modern people are also religious – we just worship money, science, progress, etc.

Key Takeaway  
The steppes functioned as the most brutal innovation engine in human history. The harshest environment produced the most open, aggressive, curious, adaptive and deadly humans – who then periodically reset the civilized world when it became fat, corrupt, static and weak.  
Civilization innovates in its city-state youth, stagnates and decays in its imperial maturity, and is periodically conquered and rejuvenated by the steppe barbarians.  
Only gunpowder ended the cycle.
