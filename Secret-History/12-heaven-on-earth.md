## Review of YouTube Comments from Last Class

- **Gary Zorakiski** on cave paintings: “It is possible they created art everywhere; the paintings only survived in caves.”  
  → Absolutely correct. Ice Age people made art as ritual and celebration, not to leave a legacy. They painted, carved, and sang everywhere — only the caves preserved it.

- **Beethoven comment**: He was not born deaf; he became deaf later.  
  → He likely had **synaesthesia** (mixing of senses). Many highly talented people see colors when they hear sounds or experience other sensory crossovers. Pitch-perfect musicians often “see” notes as colors.

- **Leonard Euler** (mathematician): Did his best work after going blind because blindness intensifies inner imagination and visualisation.

- **Indigenous people produce no trash**: True for almost all of human history. We saw ourselves as part of nature; leaving trash would be like trashing your own home.

## Core Concepts: Debunking Modern Myths About Human Nature

We do not primarily learn facts or dates in this class — we learn **ideas and concepts**.

### Myth 1: Humans are materialistic (we want money, sex, power)

**Truth**: For 95–99 % of our history our three deepest impulses were:

1. Religious/spiritual expression (art, music, dance) to answer: Where do we come from? Why are we here? Where are we going?
2. Desire to differentiate ourselves and stand out
3. Curiosity and the joy of exploration

We are taught materialism because it makes us easier to control and more obedient workers.

### Myth 2: The nuclear family is the most natural human unit

**Truth**: Most ancient societies were matriarchal or matrilineal.  
Women had full bodily autonomy → controlled sex → controlled power.  
They often chose multiple partners because:

- It’s pleasurable
- Sex is an excellent political tool (turns enemies into friends)
- Hiding paternity makes **all** men in the community invest in every child (“village raises the child”)

Result: highly egalitarian societies focused on harmony, not status.  
We switched to patriarchy to maximise reproduction (women became reproductive slaves). Population exploded, but women’s status and health collapsed.

### Myth 3: Humans believe in “survival of the fittest”

**Truth**: We have always been deeply compassionate — caring for the sick, elderly, and weak. Teaching “survival of the fittest” creates fear and anxiety, which makes people work harder and be easier to control.

### Myth 4: Humans have gotten smarter over time

**Truth**: We have always been just as intelligent; we simply express it differently.  
Primitive people had superior memory, sensitivity, resilience, and imagination.

### Myth 5: We evolved from apes (mentally and culturally)

**Truth**: Our bodies may have evolved, but our minds and imagination did not.  
We can consciously choose our evolution.  
Teaching ape-origin justifies hierarchy (“the powerful are just more fit”).

## Proof of Ancient Intelligence: Polynesian Navigation

- Settled the entire Pacific on small boats without instruments
- Memorised star maps, ocean currents, wind patterns, wave signatures, bird migrations, and individual island ecosystems — all in their heads
- Navigators could not sleep for days or they would die
- Today we need computers for the same task

This is raw human imagination in action.

## The Pyramids (c. 2600–2400 BCE) — Not Tombs, Temples

- Not built by aliens or Atlantis — racist and confirmation-bias nonsense
- Built entirely through human imagination: visualised completely in the mind, constructed from the inside out, no blueprints
- Massive public works project to bring heaven to earth
- Took ~20 years and hundreds of thousands of workers united by one shared religious vision

We literally could not build the Great Pyramid today, even with all our machines, because:

- We lack shared religious purpose (“make my grandchildren’s grandchildren prosperous forever”)
- We lack collective visionary imagination (they saw the finished structure in their heads and transmitted it almost telepathically)
- We use top-down, process-oriented management instead of shared vision
- We do the least work for the most pay; they did the most careful work to create eternity

Modern motto: fast, cheap, good enough  
Ancient motto: eternal, perfect, godly

## Other Examples of Bringing Heaven to Earth

- **Hagia Sophia** (537 CE, Constantinople) — still standing, breathtaking
- **Aachen Cathedral** (792–800 CE, Germany) — still perfect today
- **Manhattan Project** (20th century) — 130,000 people united by the vision of ending large-scale war forever (and they largely succeeded — no WWIII yet)

## Göbekli Tepe (c. 9600 BCE) — The World’s First Known Temple

- Built by hunter-gatherers before agriculture
- Enormous T-shaped pillars (up to 20 ft tall, 20 tons)
- Proves humans are religious first — we build temples wherever we go

### The Universal Temple-Building Cycle (repeats throughout history)

1. Charismatic leader has a divine vision → shares it → everyone works joyfully
2. Temple becomes the most valuable real estate on earth (people bring tribute)
3. Leader dies → succession crisis → factions, secret societies, power struggles
4. Often settled by human sacrifice (archaeological evidence at Göbekli Tepe)

## Other Early Sites

- **Karahan Tepe** — nearby, skull cult / ancestor worship
- **Tower of Jericho** — aligned so sun at equinox casts dramatic shadow; religious, not defensive
- **Çatalhöyük** (c. 7500–5700 BCE) — first permanent settlement  
  → People chose farming (harder, less healthy life) for religious reasons — to stay near their gods  
  → Each house was a private temple; bull + mother goddess worship  
  → Egalitarian, artistic, sophisticated

## The Life Cycle of Early Settlements (e.g., Çatalhöyük)

1. Charismatic leader → messianic vision → beautiful, artistic, egalitarian community
2. Population grows → more farming → environmental stress + climate change
3. Leadership becomes hereditary → corruption → exploitation
4. People remember the original vision → simply walk away and start a new community

You can always vote with your feet.

## Three Fundamental Laws of Human Societies

1. Societies are fluid and dynamic — always changing
2. Diversity **within** societies is greater than diversity **between** societies
3. Communities exist in opposition to each other (they deliberately differentiate)

## Recommended Reading Highlights

### The Dawn of Everything – David Graeber & David Wengrow

- Many societies consciously switched social structures seasonally or situationally
- Example: Northwest Coast natives — hierarchical aristocracy in winter, egalitarian in summer; people even used different names in each season

### The Wayfinder – Wade Davis

Amazon indigenous mythologies are extraordinarily complex and sophisticated despite no writing.  
Everything is alive, everything has spiritual essence, shaman negotiates with animal masters before hunting — exactly like cave paintings 30,000 years earlier.

### The Forest People – Colin Turnbull (Mbuti Pygmies)

- The forest is a living benevolent parent
- When bad things happen it is because “the forest is sleeping”
- They sing all night to wake the forest so it will look after its children again
- If someone falls asleep during the ritual or disrespects it → they are killed and erased from memory

Same core idea everywhere: maintain harmony, reciprocity, and connection with the living world.
