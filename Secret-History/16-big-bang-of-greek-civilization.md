## Historical Context: From Empire to Polis

Let us quickly review where we are.

Early cities emerge on major trade routes beside great rivers. As they grow, they create colonies along the river, leading to warfare among city-states for control of trade. This open, cooperative competition drives tremendous innovation (Mesopotamia, Egypt, China).

Eventually an empire emerges → bureaucracy → three fatal characteristics:

1. Centralisation → monopoly → end of competition → decreased innovation
2. Censorship → no free expression, all information controlled
3. Writing used as propaganda and control rather than knowledge creation or self-expression

This happened in Mycenaean (“Minoan-Mycenaean”) Greece during the Bronze Age.

When the Mycenaean empire collapsed, the system flipped and produced the miracle of classical Greece.

## The Revolution of the Polis

- Centralisation disappeared → replaced by the **polis** (city-state) → root of the word “politics”
- Constant warfare between poleis → every citizen had to risk his life → every citizen gained the right to speak in assembly
- Citizens (even farmers) had to educate themselves, learn rhetoric, gain knowledge
- Massive education + competition → explosion of innovation

Because every citizen now had to read and write, the old elite-only script (Linear B) became obsolete.  
Linear B was deliberately difficult so only the palace elite could use it (propaganda model).

The Greeks created the **alphabet** — the most efficient writing system ever invented.

### Evolution of Writing Systems

1. Pictograms → picture of the thing (sun, moon)
2. Ideograms → symbol represents a concept
3. Syllabary → symbol represents a syllable (Linear B, Chinese characters are partly ideographic)
4. Alphabet → symbol represents a single phoneme (part of a sound) + Greeks added **vowels** → revolutionary efficiency

Result: mass literacy → entertainment → professional bards/poets → **Homer**

In a centralized empire Homer would have been a propagandist.  
In the decentralized, competitive Greek world he became the greatest poet humanity has ever produced and is rightly called the father of Greek civilization.

## Homer and the Trojan War Tradition

Homer was likely blind and illiterate; he recited, students wrote it down.  
Both epics concern the legendary Trojan War.

Troy controlled the entrance to the Black Sea → centre of world trade → constantly raided by Greek pirates. Facts → legends → exaggeration over centuries → Trojan War myth.

### The Judgment of Paris (cause of the war)

- Golden apple inscribed “To the fairest”
- Hera → power (kingdom)
- Athena → wisdom
- Aphrodite → the world’s most beautiful woman (Helen)  
  Paris chooses Aphrodite → marries Helen (already married to Menelaus, king of Sparta).  
  Menelaus’ brother Agamemnon, king of Mycenae, leads pan-Greek expedition to retrieve her.  
  War lasts ten years.

Main heroes:

- Achilles – greatest warrior
- Odysseus – wisest strategist (Trojan Horse)

## The Iliad – Plot Summary

The _Iliad_ does **not** narrate the whole war; it covers only a few weeks in the tenth year and focuses on the quarrel between Achilles and Agamemnon.

1. Agamemnon seizes Achilles’ war-prize girl (Briseis) after returning his own (Chryseis) to stop Apollo’s plague
2. Achilles withdraws from battle in rage → Greeks begin losing badly
3. Achilles asks his goddess-mother Thetis to persuade Zeus to favour Troy temporarily so the Greeks will beg him to return
4. Trojans, led by Hector, almost burn the Greek ships
5. Patroclus (Achilles’ best friend) begs to fight in Achilles’ armour → pushes Trojans back but is killed by Hector
6. Achilles, mad with grief and guilt, re-enters battle, kills Hector, mutilates the body
7. Priam, guided by Hermes, visits Achilles’ tent at night, kisses the hands that killed his son, begs for Hector’s body
8. Achilles, moved to tears, returns the body and grants 12-day truce for funeral  
   → The poem ends not with victory but with mutual grief and reconciliation

## The True Theme: Forgiveness and the Human Heart

Achilles should be happiest man alive (greatest hero, avenged friend) yet falls into deepest depression.  
Why? He knows, deep down, that \*\*he is guilty of Patroclus’ death.

Achilles’ three fatal mistakes:

1. Petty quarrel with Agamemnon
2. Refusing the embassy’s gifts and plea
3. Sending Patroclus into battle but forbidding him to take full glory (reverse psychology → Patroclus overreaches)

Achilles cannot forgive himself → projects rage onto Hector’s corpse → cannot sleep, cannot eat.

Priam’s act of supreme courage (humbling himself before his son’s killer) allows Achilles to finally weep and forgive himself.  
Both men weep together — for Patroclus, Hector, their fathers, their own mortality.

The real battlefield is the human heart.  
The greatest problem in any society: how to forgive others and how to forgive ourselves.

Homer shows that forgiveness rejuvenates the world.

## Julian Jaynes and the Bicameral Mind

Why were the ancient Greeks capable of such profound wisdom while we struggle today?

Julian Jaynes (_The Origin of Consciousness in the Breakdown of the Bicameral Mind_):

- Right brain hemisphere receives “vibrations” from the universe (Kant’s noumena, Hegel’s Geist)
- Left brain hemisphere interprets them into phenomena (everyday reality)
- Until ≈ 1200–800 BCE most humans were “bicameral”: the right-brain “voice” was heard as gods/ancestors commanding them
- Stress/decision → hallucinated divine voice gave automatic guidance
- Collapse of Bronze Age civilizations → breakdown of bicameral mind → modern conscious, metaphorical mind emerges

Evidence in the _Iliad_:

- Heroes constantly receive direct commands from gods (Athena physically grabs Achilles)
- No modern interior monologue; decisions come as external divine voices
- The gods = personified emotions and natural forces

Modern mind has shut off the right hemisphere (“only science and logic matter”) → loss of wisdom, meaning, creativity.

## The Iliad vs Modern Prose – A Direct Comparison

Modern prose version of Achilles–Agamemnon clash (my writing):

> Achilles’ blood boiled and he reached for the hilt of his sword… His feet grew heavy and his legs grew wobbly. He could not move… Achilles snapped around and walked out.

Factual, but gives no insight.

Homer (Fagles translation):

> Down from the vaulting heaven swept Athena… Only Achilles saw her… “I come to check your rage, if only you will yield…”

Truthful — shows the psychological mechanism.

Same with Achilles’ speech to Patroclus and the final meeting with Priam: Homer uses gods to reveal inner psychic conflict modern prose hides.

## Greek Oral Culture – Daily Practice of the Heart

- Constant public speaking (assembly, law courts, theater, symposium, agora debate)
- Theater tragedies explore human heart (Aeschylus, Sophocles, Euripides)
- Writing still communal — authors recited works publicly first  
  → Everyone constantly examined what it means to be human → greatest civilization in history

## Modern Literature After “Killing God”

When we lost the spiritual dimension we clutter the mind with trivialities searching for significance.

Anna Karenina (Tolstoy) translated into heroic/Homeric style becomes far more powerful: Anna hallucinates her future lonely self at the train station — she understands her fate and throws herself under the train anyway.

Virginia Woolf’s stream-of-consciousness (considered great modern literature) shows only fragmented, schizophrenic modern mind with no depth or discipline.
