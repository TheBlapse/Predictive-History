# Secret History #15: Capital and the Bronze Age Collapse

## Introduction: The Four River Valley Civilizations

The four earliest major civilizations — Egypt (Nile), Mesopotamia (Tigris-Euphrates), Indus Valley (Indus), and China (Yellow River) — arose because great rivers enabled large-scale agriculture and long-distance trade. Each developed a dominant city that established colonies along the river to control resources and trade routes. Competition for these routes led to constant warfare, which in turn drove technological innovation.

## The Invention of Bronze and the Globalization of Trade

Wars demanded better weapons → bronze (copper + tin alloy) became the strongest metal available → ideal for swords, shields, and armor.

The critical problem: tin deposits are rare and scattered across the world (Central Asia, Cornwall, Iberia, Bohemia, Afghanistan, etc.).  
To obtain tin, civilizations had to build ever-longer, ever-more-complex trade routes.  
Trade routes themselves became cities and new centers of power.  
The entire known world became interconnected solely to feed the war machine.

Result: the Late Bronze Age (c. 1600–1200 BCE) became the first truly globalized era in human history.

## The Exception: The Peaceful Indus Valley Civilization

Archaeology in the Indus Valley (modern Pakistan/northwest India) has uncovered:

- Advanced urban planning
- Standardized housing
- Sophisticated drainage and hygiene systems
- No palaces, no temples of obvious kings/priests, no weapons of war

Bronze existed, but was used for jewelry, pottery, and status symbols — not weapons.  
The civilization appears peaceful, egalitarian, artistic, and deeply religious.  
Yet it was fully participated in the global bronze trade → bronze shifted from a weapon to capital.

## The Sudden Collapse (c. 1200 BCE)

In roughly 50 years, the entire system collapsed:

- Shang Dynasty China ends
- Mycenaean Greece destroyed
- Hittite Empire destroyed
- Egypt invaded repeatedly by “Sea Peoples,” survives but is exhausted and loses great-power status
- Indus Valley cities abandoned or overwhelmed by refugees from the steppes
- Troy destroyed (Trojan War era)

Mesopotamia proved most resilient because it had always been open to invasion and regime change (Akkadian → Babylonian → Assyrian cycles) → it was used to chaos.

## The Perfect Storm of Crises (Eric Cline’s 1177 BCE)

- Climate change → drought → famine
- Mass migration / refugee crises
- Earthquakes
- Internal rebellions
- Collapse of trade → tin and copper supplies cut → bronze weapons/armor unavailable → military collapse
- Sea Peoples (refugees + pirates) overwhelm coastal civilizations

## The Deeper Cause: The Nature of Capital

The lecturer’s central argument: the collapse was not just bad luck; it was the inevitable consequence of the world’s first proto-capitalist system.

### What Makes Something Capital?

1. Universal desire (everyone wants it)  
   2 Store of value (wealth can be parked in it)  
   3 Mobility (can be transported at low cost)

Bronze satisfied all three → became the first true global currency (comparable to the US dollar today).

Historical examples that failed as capital:

- Grain (not mobile, spoils)
- Cattle (not easily movable)
- Slaves (value unstable, rebellious)
  → Bronze → Gold → US dollar

### The Two Human Natures

- Altruistic nature: cares about relationships, harmony, reputation
- Utilitarian nature: cares about profit, power, efficiency

Capital shifts society from altruistic to utilitarian.

Initial effect: explosive growth, innovation, exploration, wealth.  
Late effect: exploitation, inequality, alienation, indifference, cheating → systemic fragility.

When the majority feel exploited and see no path upward → they stop participating → system collapses from within even before external shocks hit.

## Archaeological Evidence of Globalization

Shipwrecks (e.g., Uluburun off Turkey) contain goods from:

- Cyprus (copper)
- Canaan
- Mycenaean Greece
- Egypt
- Baltic amber
- Afghan tin
- Caspian Sea materials

→ proves single ships carried products from the entire known world.

Trade routes expanded relentlessly outward until every tin source was connected.

## Strategic Choke Points and Empire

The best money was made not by mining or manufacturing, but by controlling trade routes (Troy, Middle East today).  
Empires in the Bronze Age were essentially armed trade networks, not modern nation-states.

## Specific Civilizations in Focus

### Indus Valley (Harappan Civilization)

- Extremely advanced hygiene, standardized bricks, equal-sized houses
- No evidence of kings, armies, or war
- Possibly consciously rejected the war-based system of the others after trading with them

### Mycenaean Greece

- Warrior aristocracy (descended from Indo-European steppe peoples)
- Palace-centered command economy (all surplus to palace → redistributed to artisans for trade goods)
- Dominated Mediterranean sea trade → piracy very profitable
- Participants in Trojan War (essentially a trade-route war)

### Palace Economy → Tombs and the Psychology of Wealth

Mycenaeans and Egyptians buried rulers with immense treasure even though “you can’t take it with you.”  
Lecturer’s explanation: extreme wealth literally changes brain chemistry → loss of empathy, obsession with immortality → blood transfusions, bunkers, secret societies, even Satanism among modern billionaires → desperate attempt to keep wealth/power forever.

## Why Satanism among the super-rich?

They know they cannot face divine judgment for how they acquired their wealth → prefer hell (or a deal with a stronger power) over accountability to God.

## Capital’s Inevitable Cycle

Capital → rapid growth → extreme inequality → alienation → indifference or revolt → collapse.  
Seen in:

- Late Bronze Age
- Pre-WWI globalized world
- Today

## After the Collapse: The Birth of Greek Civilization

Mycenaean palace system collapses → Greek Dark Ages → emergence of the polis (independent city-state)  
Writing changes from Linear B (bureaucratic) → Phoenician-derived alphabet  
End of censorship → Homer and epic poetry become possible  
Warrior aristocracy + competitive city-states + alphabetic literacy + individual expression = Greek miracle

Main lesson: Collapse is devastating, but it destroys rigid, exploitative systems and creates space for something new and greater to emerge.

The Bronze Age had to die for Greek (and thus Western) civilization to be born.

We may be living in a similar moment today.
