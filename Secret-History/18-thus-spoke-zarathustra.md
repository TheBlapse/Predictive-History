# Secret History #18: Thus Spoke Zarathustra

## Introduction: The Most Influential Person Who Ever Lived

We have studied the Greeks and the Israelites. Today we turn to the Persians, and I introduce the most influential human being who has ever lived: **Zoroaster** (Zarathustra).

Zoroaster created the world’s first great universal religion — **Zoroastrianism** — which in turn made possible the world’s first great empire: the **Achaemenid Persian Empire**.

## The Intuitive Human Understanding of the Universe

From the very beginning, human beings have understood reality in essentially the same way:

- The universe is one of consciousness.
- Everything is constantly vibrating.
- The slower the vibration, the denser it becomes → matter → bodies → us.
- We inhabit both the material and the spiritual universe simultaneously.
- When we vibrate (think, feel, act), our consciousness returns to and dialogues with the universe.

Because this is difficult to grasp directly, we have always used metaphors and stories.

### The Metaphor of the Monad (The One)

God = the Monad / the One / Nous / Ahura Mazda.  
God is perfect.  
The problem with perfection is the lack of imagination and creativity.

Therefore God emanates → creates imperfect, material beings (us) who can hit walls, feel pain, make mistakes, fall, suffer, and die.  
It is precisely through fallibility, disobedience, and mistake-making that creativity arises.

Thus God and humans are **co-creators** in an eternal process of becoming — becoming perfect, becoming infinite, becoming eternal.

### Essential Features of the System

1. **Free will** – Without total freedom there can be no true creativity.
2. **The individual** – What matters is what happens inside each person; only the individual consciousness can generate genuine creativity.  
   Metaphor: We are mirrors surrounding a single candle (the Monad). The light is reflected in each of us uniquely.
3. **Eternity / Reincarnation** – Consciousness returns to the spirit world after death for reflection.
   - Good, virtuous lives → ascension to higher planes (closer to the Monad).
   - Evil lives → lower realms where one must confront the pain one caused.  
     This is the origin of our concepts of heaven and hell.

## The Corruption of the Bronze Age and the Need for Prophets

As populations grew → war → patriarchy → property → capital.  
The Bronze Age reached the height of capital and therefore the height of war, slavery, corruption, violence, and evil.

In every such dark age, **prophets** appear to remind humanity of our divine origin and purpose: to celebrate life, to create, to love — not to destroy.

Zoroaster is one of these prophets (like Homer, like the Yahwist). He appears at the end of the Bronze Age in a world that can no longer imagine life without war, patriarchy, and property, and he offers a radical new vision.

## Zoroastrianism: The Core Revolution

In a polytheistic world where gods are tribal and amoral, Zoroaster proclaims:

- There is one supreme God: **Ahura Mazda** (“Lord of Wisdom”), represented by fire → the religion of light/fire.
- The cosmic battle is not between tribes but **within each human heart**:  
  **Asha** (truth, righteousness, virtue, order, justice)  
  versus  
  **Druj** (the Lie, deception, destruction).

The Greeks said of the Persians: they excel at three things — riding horses, shooting arrows, and **telling the truth**. Truth is not mere honesty; **Asha** is the active, virtuous life that aligns the individual with Ahura Mazda.

### Zoroaster’s Teaching ≈ Kant’s Categorical Imperative (shown 2,500 years later)

Kant’s three formulations of the moral law are almost identical to Asha:

1. **Universality**  
   Act only according to that maxim whereby you can at the same time will that it should become a universal law.  
   → Imagine everyone in the world instantly does exactly what you are about to do. Would you still do it?

2. **Free will**  
   Act only from freely chosen duty, never from coercion or compulsion.

3. **Humans as ends, never as means**  
   Never sacrifice individuals for any supposed “greater good.”

These three principles produce the most revolutionary ideas in human history:

- The sovereign **individual**
- **Free choice**
- **Truth** (known in the heart, not imposed by family, tribe, or state)  
  → and ultimately **ethical monotheism**.

Zoroastrianism directly births Judaism, Christianity, and Islam (2–3 billion people today).

## Plato’s Allegory of the Cave — A Zoroastrian Teaching

Plato revered Zoroaster as the first philosopher/scientist/magician. The Allegory of the Cave is essentially a retelling of Asha:

Prisoners chained in a cave see only shadows and mistake them for reality.  
One breaks free, ascends into the sunlight (Asha / Ahura Mazda), is blinded at first, then sees the real world in its beauty.  
True enlightenment is not complete until the escaped prisoner returns to the cave to free the others — even though they will call him mad and try to kill him.

**Asha is therefore not only personal salvation; it is the responsibility to spread light.**

## Historical Context of Zoroaster

Likely lived 2000–1000 BCE, toward the **end** of the Bronze Age (when the system had become most corrupt).  
Probably northern or northeastern Iran — a mining colony supplying tin to Mesopotamia/Egypt/Levant.  
Child slavery, extreme violence, total exploitation — the Congo of the ancient world.  
Zoroaster, a priest himself, is disgusted by corrupt, mercenary priests and wanders as a singing poet-prophet reminding people of their divine spark.

Key line he taught:  
“Listen with your own ears, with a bright mind. Choose truth from false creed. Each person for his own self before the final judgment comes.”

## The Gathas (Zoroaster’s Own Hymns)

> The two primal spirits who reveal themselves in vision as twins are the Better and the Bad in thought, word, and action. Between these two the wise choose rightly, the foolish not so.

> Of these twin spirits, the Lie chose doing the worst things; the Most Holy Spirit, clothing Himself with the massy heavens as garment, chose Right.

> Good thoughts, good words, good deeds — these three must be unified in the follower of Asha.

> Happiness belongs to the one who follows the righteous path for the sake of righteousness itself.

> Even in a world of evil you can — and must — choose to be good.

## Echoes Across Time

### Rumi (13th century CE — the “reincarnation” of Zoroaster in Persian poetry)

> I see so deeply with myself, not needing my eyes…  
> Not Christian or Jew or Muslim, not Hindu, Buddhist, Sufi, or Zen…  
> My place is placeless, a trace of the traceless. Neither body nor soul.  
> I belong to the Beloved, have seen the two worlds as One.

### Friedrich Nietzsche — Thus Spoke Zarathustra (1883–1885)

Nietzsche felt literally seized by Zoroaster and wrote the entire book as Zarathustra’s words. Key passages:

- Zarathustra descends from his mountain cave after ten years of solitude, overflowing with wisdom, needing hands outstretched to receive it.
- “Out of your poisons you brewed balsam… all your devils became angels.”
- Virtue arises only through struggle with vice.
- “One must have chaos in oneself to give birth to a dancing star.”
- The true seeker must burn in his own flame to become new.
- “Man is something that shall be overcome.”

Nietzsche/Zoroaster rejects organized religion’s gravity and fear, instead, calls for joyous, dancing, creative life-affirmation.

## Q&A Highlights

**Student**: If Hitler believed in his heart he was doing justice, was that his Asha?  
**Answer**: Ahura Mazda is infinite forgiveness. In the end **everyone** is forgiven. There is no eternal hell; hell is only what we create in our hearts here. Asha means stop judging others and transform yourself. What matters is **your** movement toward truth, not comparison with others. Ego and fear of rejection are what block Asha.

**Student**: Does Asha change over a person’s lifetime?  
**Answer**: Yes. Asha/God is creativity. You must constantly reinvent yourself. There is no final arrival — only endless becoming. That is why reincarnation exists: we keep returning until we help raise the entire world.

**Student**: So we never actually “reach” Ahura Mazda?  
**Answer**: Correct. Perfection is impossible, because as soon as you approached it you would have the duty to return and help others. The meaning of existence is the eternal struggle toward light. The universe is process, struggle, creativity — and that is beautiful.
