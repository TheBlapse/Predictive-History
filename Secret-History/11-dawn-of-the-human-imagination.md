## Charles Darwin and the Theory of Evolution

Charles Darwin is arguably the most influential thinker of the last 200 years because he gave us the theory of evolution in _On the Origin of Species_ (1859).

Three core pillars of the theory:

- It is **accidental** → genetic mutations are random; the one best fitted to the environment survives.
- It is **materialistic** → only what you can see and measure exists.
- It is **emergent** → complexity builds bottom-up from simple accidental changes.

In only 20 years this idea conquered the entire Western world — schools, universities, sciences — and became the unquestionable paradigm. Today, to doubt evolution is to be labelled “crazy.”

## Problems with the Standard Evolutionary Story

We are taught:  
apes → multiple human species → only Homo sapiens wins → we are just clever monkeys whose main drives are food, sex, and dominance.

Yet huge anomalies appear:

- Why is there only **one** surviving human species today when evolution predicts tremendous diversity?
- Why do humans migrate across oceans and continents when no other primate does? (Polynesian voyages across the Pacific are suicidal unless you already _know_ islands are there.)
- Why are Neanderthals (supposedly “primitive”) performing complex burial rituals 100,000+ years ago?
- Why are Homo sapiens spending enormous time and danger deep inside dark caves painting breathtaking art that no one will see? Art is useless for survival — it actually wastes calories.

These behaviours make no sense in a purely materialist, survival-of-the-fittest framework.

## Ice Age Humanity: Intuitive, Telepathic, Collective Genius

Early humans did not need bosses, plans, or language the way we think. They simply **knew**.

Thought experiment:  
Ten people, no talking, no planning, one blank wall. Draw a picture together.  
You expect chaos. Instead, something magical happens — one person draws a sun, another instantly adds a tree, another a mountain, another animals. A coherent, beautiful composition emerges without a single word. This is how Ice Age people built mammoth-bone houses, organized hunts, created art — through collective intuition, empathy, almost telepathy.

They changed social organization seasonally and intuitively: sometimes bands of 5–10, sometimes gatherings of hundreds or thousands for religious festivals — all without calendars or leaders. If you didn’t like your group, you simply walked away and joined or started another.

This was the most diverse, dynamic, creative period of human existence.

## Cave Art (40,000–12,000 years ago)

- Painted with red ochre, charcoal, in deep, dangerous, oxygen-poor caves.
- Not childish scribbles — masterful, lifelike, surreal, mythological works showing profound care and devotion.
- Often narrative: horses running from lions → cycle of life and death, destruction and renewal.
- Animals are commemorated after being killed — gratitude, balance, harmony with nature.
- Shamans appear (e.g., the “Lion-Man” figurine, 40,000 years old, Germany).
- Bird symbols (representing Mother Earth) herding animals.
- Caves = portals between material and spirit worlds. Painting inside them summoned animal spirits to return → completing the cycle of life.

Artists painted in near-darkness, low-oxygen states → trance states → divine inspiration. They believed they were **channeling** the divine, not inventing from personal experience.

## Symbols and the Deliberate Rejection of Writing

Ice Age art contains dozens of recurring geometric signs catalogued by Genevieve von Petzinger.  
These constitute a proto-writing system — yet writing was consciously rejected because:

- True speech is song and divine channeling; writing counterfeits the divine.
- Music and ritual cannot be captured in text.
- Everything must be communal; writing is solitary.

Writing was not “invented later” later — it was deliberately avoided for tens of thousands of years as a corruption.
