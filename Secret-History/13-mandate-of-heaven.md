# Secret History #13: Mandate of Heaven

## 1. The Traditional (Marxist) Understanding of Civilization (What You Were Taught in School)

The standard story goes like this:

- Hunter-gatherer life was miserable → constant search for food, starvation, short lives.
- Invention of agriculture → surplus food.
- Surplus → some people no longer have to farm → a leisure class (elite) emerges.
- This elite has time to invent religion, art, music, dance, painting, science, and technology.
- With surplus + elite innovation, society can now support:
  - Cities (thanks to irrigation and river control)
  - Hereditary elites who focus full-time on progress
  - Writing (only the elite have time to learn it)
  - Money and property

These four elements (cities, hereditary elite, writing, money/property) = Civilization.

Benefits: religion, art, science.  
Costs: war, slavery, debt.

This is the dominant academic view and it is essentially Marxist.

**Problem with this view**: It assumes prehistoric humans were stupid and incapable of art, religion, or complex thought without a leisure class. Yet we have 40,000-year-old cave paintings and Göbekli Tepe (11,000 BCE) – monumental religious architecture built before agriculture.

## 2. The Alternative (and More Compelling) Framework

Humans were always religious, artistic, and capable of science and complex organization. We never needed an elite to “give” us these things.

Evidence:

- Cave paintings
- Göbekli Tepe and other pre-agricultural religious centers

Sequence of development:

1. People gather for religious purposes → build temples/monuments (e.g., Göbekli Tepe).
2. Farming develops around these temples to feed the pilgrims and priests.
3. Temples start as egalitarian or elected leadership serving the people.
4. Over time, temple leadership becomes hereditary and corrupt → rent-seeking.
5. When it gets too bad, people simply leave and build a new temple elsewhere.
6. BUT in locations with supreme trade value (strategic real estate), people cannot easily leave → city grows massively.
7. Temple becomes the center of a “temple economy” → primal taxation: everyone brings food, priests redistribute → enables public works (irrigation, more temples).
8. Need for record-keeping → writing is invented (to track contributions, rations, trade).
9. Money emerges from trade.
10. Strict hierarchy solidifies.
11. To prevent people from leaving, the elite creates mythology that portrays the hierarchy as divinely ordained (gaslighting).

**Core idea**: Civilization did not give us religion, art, and science. We already had them. Civilization is a trap – a propaganda device to lock people into an illegitimate hierarchy they would otherwise abandon.

## 3. The Four Earliest Major Civilizations – Why These Four?

They share three crucial characteristics:

1. Same latitude (not too hot, not too cold) → ideal for agriculture.
2. Located on major rivers → water, transport, fertile soil:
   - Egypt → Nile
   - Mesopotamia → Tigris & Euphrates
   - Indus Valley (Harappan) → Indus
   - China → Yellow River
3. Access to the sea/ocean → trade, new goods, people, ideas:
   - Egypt → Mediterranean + Red Sea
   - Mesopotamia → Persian Gulf → Indian Ocean
   - Indus → Arabian Sea → Indian Ocean
   - China → Pacific (but largely blocked by Himalayas → China is a special case)

These locations turned small settlements into massive trade hubs → colonies upstream/downstream → full civilizations.

The first three (Egypt, Mesopotamia, Indus) were in constant contact via sea trade → they form the integrated foundation of what we call “Western civilization” (not just Europe + America).

## 4. The Most Strategically Located Place on Earth: Sumer (Southern Mesopotamia)

- Center of global trade routes
- Connects Egypt, Indus, Anatolia, Central Asia, Levant, Europe, Africa
- Extremely fertile (Fertile Crescent)
- First major civilization
- Invented writing (cuneiform), large-scale irrigation, many technologies
- Rapid development puzzles scholars → explanation: when humans are thrown together for trade and religion, they innovate extremely quickly (“necessity is the mother of invention”)

Sumerian language is an isolate → possibly a trade pidgin/creole formed by diverse groups meeting there.

Ziggurats = temple platforms, center of city life, home of the god, accessible only to priests → control mechanism.

## 5. The Great Historical Principle: Constant Inversion

Human history is a continuous process of overthrowing and inverting the old order:

- Hunter-gatherer → egalitarian, fluid, animistic (everything contains god)
- Early agricultural → Mother Goddess religion (fertility, compassion, balance)
- Larger societies + warfare → rise of male Sky God (struggle, toil, exploitation of earth and people)
- Hereditary kings → civil wars, son kills father (parricide)
- Over-populated bureaucracies → bureaucrats steal power from king (“the servant rules the master”)

## 6. From Mother Goddess to Sky God

Mother Goddess era: kindness, harmony, fertility, respect for nature.  
Sky God era (Ra, Marduk, Zeus, Jupiter): demands sacrifice, discipline, conquest, exploitation of nature and people → perfect ideological justification for irrigation societies that control and exploit the land.

## 7. Enuma Elish – Babylonian Creation Epic (Propaganda in Stone)

Written on clay tablets (durable → we still have it).  
Purpose: justify Babylonian hierarchy as divinely ordained.

Story summary:

- Primordial gods: Apsu (fresh water) + Tiamat (salt water) → give birth to younger gods.
- Younger gods are noisy → Apsu wants to kill them → Tiamat warns them → they kill Apsu.
- Tiamat, enraged, attacks with monster army.
- Younger gods choose Marduk (thunder/sky god) as champion.
- Marduk kills Tiamat, splits her body like a fish:
  - Upper half → heavens
  - Lower half → earth
- Marduk creates calendar, constellations, bureaucratic order.
- Gods want rest → Marduk creates humans from the blood of Tiamat’s general (Qingu) to serve as slaves to the gods.
- Anunnaki gods divided into upper (heaven) and lower (earth) groups → hierarchy.
- Babylon is declared the divine city built by the gods → you cannot leave; servitude is holy.

Core message: Struggle, exploitation, hierarchy, bureaucracy, and slavery are the divine order. The old compassionate Mother Goddess religion is dead.

## 8. Epic of Gilgamesh – The First Great Literary Work

- Gilgamesh = tyrannical king of Uruk.
- People complain → gods create wild Enkidu.
- Enkidu civilized by prostitute → fights Gilgamesh → they become best friends.
- They kill divine beings → gods kill Enkidu as punishment.
- Gilgamesh quests for immortality → fails → returns home and finds meaning in serving his people and building the city.

Possible purpose: teach that true kingship = serving the people so they will remember and celebrate you forever.

## 9. How Myths Are Constructed and Consolidated (The Bureaucratic Editing Process)

1. Local legends → exaggerated oral traditions.
2. Regional consolidation → heroes combined (Gilgamesh = multiple older heroes).
3. Bureaucratic priests take control → add morality, humility, service to control king and people.
4. Different priestly factions fight → different versions of the story.
5. Final versions become less exciting but more useful for social control (compare Chinese classics – originally wild, later sanitized by Confucian bureaucrats).

Same process seen in Hesiod’s Theogony (Gaia → Uranus → Cronus → Zeus) and the origin stories of Sargon of Akkad, Romulus & Remus, King David, Genghis Khan, etc.

## 10. Dynastic Cycle Reflected in Myth

1. High priestess (Mother Goddess) + consort → hereditary elite → corruption.
2. Warlord/prince kills consort, marries priestess.
3. Warlord’s son relies on low-born foreign mercenary general.
4. Mercenary overthrows and becomes king → needs legitimacy myth (secret royal birth like Zeus).

## 11. Debate Between Sheep and Grain – Propaganda to Force People into Farming

Pastoralists (sheep/goat herders) = free, mobile, strong, happy.  
Farmers = stationary, taxable, controllable.

Solution: write a myth where Grain defeats Sheep in debate before the gods → farming is divinely superior.

Writing was invented primarily for administration and propaganda, not because people were suddenly “smart enough.”

## Q&A Highlights

Q: How much did Egypt, Mesopotamia, and Indus influence each other’s myths?  
A: Impossible to quantify precisely. They were in contact, but elites also wanted differentiation and superiority. Myth-making to justify hierarchy would emerge anyway; contact just adds flavor.

Q: Why is the Chinese creator god Pangu male while many early creator gods are female?  
A: Original gods tend to be asexual or hermaphroditic (yin-yang balance). Over time, as male sky-god hierarchies dominate, myths are edited to make the creator male.
